The three of you will prepare and deliver one joint presentation exploring
connections among control, inference, transport, thermodynamics, and learning.
Use Blumenthal et al., *Bridging Control,
Inference, Transport, and Thermodynamics*, as your starting point:
[read online](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.15897v1) or
[download the PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.15897).

Each student takes the lead on a topic that interests them. Work together to
explore what these fields have in common and how their ideas illuminate one
another. Go beyond summarizing the review: teach the underlying ideas and
mathematics, pursue connections you find interesting, and bring your own
scientific perspective. The topics, depth, examples, and organization are yours
to decide.

Explore how these connections appear in machine learning, data science, or
scientific computing through a scientific problem or method that interests you.

## Presentation

Present on **Thursday, December 10**. Allow roughly **15 minutes per student
plus 5 minutes together** for your shared question and Python example, followed
by discussion.

Choose a shared computational example that helps connect your topics. Each
student should also bring a brief science application from their own interests
(about **2–3 minutes within their 15 minutes**). Libraries are welcome;
**JAX or PyTorch are encouraged** where useful. You choose the problems and
how to explore them.

## AI and authorship

AI can be a brainstorming partner, tutor, or sparring partner as you explore.
Its use is optional. **Make your own slides and own the explanations you
present.** AI-generated images are welcome with judgment and acknowledgment.
Check claims and results, credit sources and meaningful AI use, and speak from
your own understanding.

## Project work and assessment

We will work on the project periodically in class. **Preparation and ongoing
project development count for 5% of the course grade**, assessed through our
work, discussions, and check-ins. Come prepared to discuss what you are
exploring and respond to questions about your developing work. The **joint
presentation and discussion count for 25%**, for **30% total**.
