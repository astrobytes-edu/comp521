Why the speed changes — Kepler’s second law. The line from star to planet sweeps equal areas in equal times. During a short time step near periastron, the line is shorter: a larger sideways step sweeps the same area. Near apastron, the line is longer and a smaller step suffices. That is why equal-time dots are farther apart near the star and closer together farther away.

Read the geometry and units

Ellipse and focus: an ellipse is a closed oval defined by two fixed points, its foci. Distances from any point on the oval to those two points add to the same total. The star is at one focus, labeled ; is the oval’s center.

Eccentricity : measures how elongated the ellipse is. Zero gives a circle; larger values below one give more elongated orbits. In a circle, all positions are the same distance from the star. We keep the same starting direction to compare the angles.

Auxiliary circle: a helper circle centered at . Its point maps vertically to the planet on the ellipse. It is a construction, not another orbiting body.

Period and angle units: means a quarter of the orbital time, not a quarter of the distance traveled. A full turn is radians or .

Orbit in space: the orbital plane contains the path. The sky plane is an imaginary image plane perpendicular to the viewing direction. Inclination is the tilt between those planes. The ascending node is where the planet crosses the sky plane toward the observer. Orientation is the angle from that node direction to closest approach, measured within the orbital plane.
