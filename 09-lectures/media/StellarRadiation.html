The photosphere is the layer from which most of a star’s visible light escapes. These are illustrative photosphere temperatures, not measured stars. A star’s outer gas layers absorb some wavelengths, producing narrow dips called absorption lines. The temperature sweep compares models; it is not a stellar-evolution simulation. Surface flux is energy emitted per unit area per unit time. Luminosity is the whole star’s emitted energy per unit time. For a spherical blackbody star, , so radius also matters for luminosity.

Spectral radiance measures radiation in a direction per unit apparent emitting area, solid angle, and wavelength. Apparent area is the surface area viewed from that direction; solid angle measures a cone of directions. The plot uses centimeter–gram–second (CGS) units; an erg is a unit of energy, and sr means steradian, the unit of solid angle. The curve is a density per unit wavelength.
