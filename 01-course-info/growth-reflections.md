# Growth Reflections

Growth Reflections are informal writing about how you learn, solve problems, and
respond when computational work becomes difficult. They are not technical
reports or polished essays. Use specific moments from your work rather than
general statements.

There is no required growth story. It is fine if something did not change, if a
project went smoothly, or if you are still confused. What matters is that you
reflect specifically and explain what the evidence shows.

:::{note} Submission format
Submit each reflection as a **PDF through the corresponding Canvas assignment**
by **11:59 pm PT** on the due date. Use a readable **12-point font**, single
spacing, and **1-inch margins**. Growth Memos 1 and 2 should each be **1–2 pages**,
with a minimum of one full page. The Growth Synthesis should be **2–3 pages**,
with a minimum of two full pages.
:::

The prompts are guides, not a checklist. Write a connected reflection rather
than answering every question mechanically. All three reflections are graded on
specificity, reflection, and evidence rather than polished prose; see
[Grading](grading.md). The AI questions are optional if you did not use AI and
required if you did.

## Growth Memo 1: Baseline

**Assigned:** Tue **Aug 25** · **Due:** Mon **Aug 31**, 11:59 pm PT

Before the course has had much chance to change how you work, reflect on where
you are now.

- Describe one piece of computational, mathematical, or scientific work you are
  proud of. What did you do well?
- How do you currently begin a coding task, and how do you decide whether your
  result is correct?
- What do you usually do when you feel stuck or confused?
- What do you expect to find most challenging in this course?
- What do you hope to get out of this course? By December, what do you want to
  understand, be able to do, or feel more confident doing?
- What will you do to help make that happen? Name two or three specific habits,
  choices, or sources of support you plan to use.

Describe where you are now rather than where you think you should be. This memo
gives you something real to compare against later.

(sec-growth-memo-2)=
## Growth Memo 2: Mid-semester

**Assigned:** Tue **Oct 6** · **Due:** Mon **Oct 19**, 11:59 pm PT

Reread Growth Memo 1 before writing. Quote or paraphrase at least one thing you
wrote there.

- What are you most proud of so far? Point to a specific piece of code, test,
  figure, explanation, or debugging moment.
- Describe a challenge you overcame. What did you try, what helped, and what did
  you learn? If the work went smoothly, explain what made your approach
  effective.
- How has your approach to planning, coding, debugging, or verification changed?
  What has not changed?
- Has your attitude toward difficulty, mistakes, asking for help, or your own
  abilities changed? It is fine if the answer is no.
- If you used AI, explain what was helpful, what was not, and what you learned
  from using it as a tutor. What remained your responsibility?
- What is one habit you want to strengthen during the rest of the semester?

(sec-growth-synthesis)=
## Growth Synthesis

**Assigned:** Tue **Nov 17** · **Due:** Mon **Dec 14**, 11:59 pm PT

Before writing, reread both earlier memos and skim your previous project
notebooks and code. Use them as evidence rather than relying only on what you
remember.

- What is the overall story of your growth this semester? Ground it in two or
  three specific moments or pieces of work.
- Where were your earlier reflections right about you? Where were they wrong?
- How has your approach to computational problems, debugging, and verification
  changed?
- What are you most proud of accomplishing?
- What was your most difficult challenge, and how did you respond?
- Has your attitude toward confusion, mistakes, difficult problems, or your own
  abilities changed? What has not changed?
- If you used AI, how did your use evolve? Explain when it helped you learn,
  when it did not, and what you learned about using it as a tutor responsibly.
- What still feels difficult or unfinished, and what practices will you carry
  into future work?
